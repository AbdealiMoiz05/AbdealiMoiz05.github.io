Apply Once: why students type the same CV into every employer's form

Applying for placements in October 2026, I typed the same work history, degree and languages into system after system, created a new account almost every time, and could not tell from half the adverts whether I was even allowed to apply. This project measures that problem properly and asks what Brunel can do about it now.

01 · The problem

The same data, typed again and again

A UK placement student who applies to 20 employers fills in 20 different forms, often across five or more different application systems. Each copy is another chance for a mistake. In one week I applied to 6 employers on 4 systems. I re-typed work history the system had just read from my CV, and lost a whole form once because it had no auto-save.

Time is only part of it. UK employers advertise placements to students on Student visas, but word the right-to-work rule so differently that the same student seems to qualify at one employer and not at the next. Students guess. A wrong guess either loses them the role or puts a false answer on a legal form.

The question: what would it take for a UK placement student to enter their application data once, correctly, and re-use it everywhere, and what can Brunel do about it this year without waiting for the software vendors?

For scale, the Institute of Student Employers survey 2025 reports a mean of 123 applications per placement hire, up 27% on the year before, and 93% of employers using online application form questions.

03 · What already exists

The idea is not new. The UK placement case is the gap.

I am not claiming to invent portable application data. The question is whether existing standards and tools cover what a UK placement student actually gets asked.

Working hypothesis, not a finding yet: each employer licenses its own copy of the software and owns its candidate data, so nobody in the chain is paid to make data travel between employers. The student carries the cost and has no seat at the table. Root-cause analysis (task 22) will test this.

EPIC 3 Requirements: what an apply-once solution must do Drafted as hypotheses 6 items

User stories with acceptance criteria. Every story starts as a hypothesis and must be linked to evidence (F-xx, R-xx, survey or interview) before it moves to Ready.

STORY 31 · High priority Hypothesis
Standard right-to-work statement for placement students

As an international student applying for a UK placement, I want a standard eligibility statement that employers recognise, so that I can answer right-to-work questions accurately.

Evidence: R-01 to R-08.

Acceptance criteria
  • Given an advert asks about right to work
  • When the student attaches the standard statement
  • Then the employer can see the placement is a required, assessed part of the degree and no sponsorship is needed
  • And the statement names the university and the visa expiry date
STORY 32 · Highest priority Hypothesis
Enter profile once, re-use everywhere

As a student, I want my education, work history and languages stored once in a portable format, so that any application form can be pre-filled.

Evidence: F-12.

Acceptance criteria
  • Given a student has a saved profile
  • When they open a new application
  • Then work history, education and languages are pre-filled
  • And every pre-filled field can be edited before submitting
STORY 33 · High priority Hypothesis
Show where a pre-filled answer came from

As a student, I want to see which earlier application a pre-filled answer came from, so that I don't submit stale answers.

Evidence: F-05.

Acceptance criteria
  • Given a field was pre-filled from an earlier application
  • When the form loads
  • Then the field is marked with its source and date
  • And the student must confirm it before submitting
STORY 34 · Medium priority Hypothesis
Record UK grades in UK format

As a UK student applying abroad, I want to give my grade in its UK form with an official explanation, so that I am not forced into a GPA conversion.

Evidence: F-02, F-03.

Acceptance criteria
  • Given a form asks for a GPA
  • When the student's institution uses UK marking
  • Then the student can enter the UK mark and classification
  • And the form shows a link to an official explanation of the scale
STORY 35 · Medium priority Hypothesis
Common 'how did you hear' list that includes university boards

As an early-careers recruiter, I want a standard source list that includes university job boards, so that I can measure which boards bring applicants.

Evidence: F-09.

Acceptance criteria
  • Given a candidate found the role on a university board
  • When they answer the source question
  • Then they can choose 'University careers service' and name the university
STORY 36 · High priority
Profile consent and data minimisation (UK GDPR)

As a student, I want to choose which parts of my profile each employer sees, so that I share only what the role needs.

Acceptance criteria
  • Given a saved profile
  • When the student applies
  • Then they see exactly which fields will be shared
  • And equality data is never shared unless they opt in for that application

07 · Working rules

How I keep this honest

Every number traces to a source. A row in the evidence log, the survey export, or a cited publication. Blank is better than guessed.

Hypotheses are labelled as hypotheses until evidence moves them to Ready.

Sources are checked before they are claimed. The Open Apply Standard has one press source so far. Until a primary source confirms it, it stays marked unverified, and if it turns out not to exist the gap analysis uses HR Open's published schemas instead.

What I got wrong so far

Two problems I first logged were not the platforms' fault. Doubled dates in one SuccessFactors form and an address that would not save on one Workday form both turned out to come from the automated typing I used to fill the forms, not from the application systems. I removed both from the log. The lesson: before blaming a system, rule out your own tools.
